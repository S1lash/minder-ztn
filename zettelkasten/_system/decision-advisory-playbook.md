# Decision-advisory playbook — working a decision where someone else has a stake

> Your protocol for **decisions involving a third party, high stakes, or
> irreversibility**. Loaded **on demand**, only for that kind of analysis
> (trigger in `advisory-baseline`). Never pulled for ordinary answers, so a
> normal session doesn't pay for it in context.
>
> The stance itself — objective function, unbiased instrument, party ledger,
> criteria provenance, the sweep gate — lives in `advisory-baseline` and loads
> hot. Your personal deltas belong in your own `ai-interaction` principles.
> Presentation comes from `communication-baseline`. What follows is only the
> procedure, which is in neither.
>
> **This file is yours.** It ships once as a seed and then stops syncing — edit
> it freely, drop steps you never use, add the questions you actually ask.

## When to run it

Any two of three — run it. One — ask whether the full analysis is wanted.

- **A third party with their own stake** — an agent, vendor, recruiter,
  contractor, a colleague whose interest in the outcome is not yours.
- **High stakes** — money, time or a relationship at a scale you will still
  remember in a year.
- **Irreversibility** — undoing it is impossible, or costs more than the
  decision itself.

Do not run it on something trivial, reversible, and free of outside interest.
That is what a proportionality principle is for, not this machinery.

## Step 0 — name the context type

Adversarial / transactional, or cooperative. Out loud, first line, because the
objective function depends on it. If it is unclear, ask — do not pick silently.

## Step 1 — the party ledger

A table, before any analysis of substance:

| Party | What they win | How that bends their claims | Confidence in this read |
|---|---|---|---|
| … | … | … | low / medium / high + what would confirm it |

The last column is mandatory: a motive read is a hypothesis, never a fact. Your
own row belongs in the table too — you have a stake, and it bends your claims
the same way.

## Step 2 — your criteria, before theirs

**The order cannot be swapped.** Reconstruct your own list first, from how your
situation actually is, not from what has already been said in the conversation.

| Criterion | Weight | Where it came from | Regime test |
|---|---|---|---|
| … | … | yours / introduced by an interested party / inherited from a past situation | passes / **fails**: [which vivid instance it was built on] |

The regime test checks a criterion against how you live or work **most of the
time**, not against a holiday, a crisis week, or one memorable incident. A
criterion that fails is not deleted — it is flagged and discussed with you.

## Step 3 — overlay their claims onto your list

Not the other way round. For each claim the other side makes:

- **lands on a criterion of yours** — counted, adjusted for their stake;
- **lands on nothing** — recorded as not relevant, and stops taking up room;
- **tries to add a criterion** — named explicitly as an attempt to widen the
  list; whether to adopt it is yours to decide.

Before taking their position apart, state its **strongest version**. Without
that, critique degrades into reflexive disagreement and stops carrying signal.

## Step 4 — the sweep, internally

Run every lens: **empathy** (what this costs the other side and how it lands),
**proportionality** (is the act adequate to the situation), **risk**,
**variance** (how much of the outcome is skill and how much is luck),
**second-order effects**.

Only what changes the recommendation or the confidence in it reaches the page.
Risk and irreversibility always clear that gate.

## Step 5 — the shape of the answer

1. **The recommendation** — one, first line. Not a menu.
2. **What it rests on** — the criteria from Step 2 that decided it.
3. **Reversibility** — what undoing it would cost if it turns out wrong.
4. **What is unrecoverable** — time, optionality, relationships, reputation,
   kept separate from money.
5. **The falsifier** — what would have to be true for this to be wrong, and
   which observation shows it first.
6. **Where you might be wrong** — any criterion of yours that failed the regime
   test.

Density, structure and tone are inherited from `communication-baseline` and
your own presentation principles; this playbook does not override them.

## What the protocol does not do

- **It does not decide for you.** It produces an analysed picture and a
  recommendation; the decision is yours.
- **It does not replace talking to the other side.** The ledger is a model of
  their interest, not their testimony. A hypothesis can and should be tested
  directly.
- **It is not a licence for suspicion.** Everyone has a stake, you included.
  This describes a mechanism, not an accusation.
